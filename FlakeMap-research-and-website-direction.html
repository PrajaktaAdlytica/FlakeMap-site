01 / What the research changes

Recommended positioning: a focused investigation workbench for understanding how tool failures change multi-step agent outcomes. The proposed value is a useful chain of evidence: injected condition → failing transition → incorrect task artifact → reviewer decision → proposed release gate.

LangSmith already documents repeated evaluations, individual repetition results and standard deviation, plus human and pairwise review. Braintrust documents multi-step evaluation, experiments, production scoring and CI workflows. Repetitions, trace-derived tests, human review and release checks cannot be presented as unique FlakeMap capabilities. LangSmith repetitions; evaluation concepts; Braintrust evaluation.

Fault injection also has direct precedent: Strands documents chaos testing with tool faults and baseline comparisons. This supports the problem category, while making differentiation harder. FlakeMap must test whether its packaging, evidence and workflow focus add value beyond the buyer’s existing tools. Strands chaos testing.

Evidence boundary: existing evaluation capabilities are documented. FlakeMap’s differentiated demand, willingness to pay, live integrations and commercial readiness are unvalidated. A polished website must not turn hypotheses into company facts.

Buyer: AI engineering lead releasing agents weekly. Champion: QA/evaluation engineer. Budget owner: engineering VP. First pilot recommendation: lead with the fault-injection module and use one purchase-order workflow to exercise the other four proposed capabilities.

03 / Three territories for the next visual exploration

These are research-stage art-direction candidates, not approved designs or generated mockups. The shared requirement is an original FlakeMap identity and a readable engineering product.

A. Failure Atlas — recommended

Warm white, dark ink, forest accents and a reserved oxide failure color. Editorial opening, precise timeline paths, small monospaced run metadata. The workflow expands into a map of divergence, then resolves into an evidence panel.

Brand character: observant, exact, calm. Strongest fit for a premium light SaaS site and the FlakeMap name.

B. Release Room

White technical surfaces, charcoal chapter breaks, amber condition markers. Two versions sit side by side; changes align by step. Composition follows a technical review desk with clear annotations and a final decision.

Brand character: rigorous, operational, accountable. Strongest emphasis on the QA champion’s daily work.

C. Replay Instrument

Ivory surfaces, graphite rails and restrained rust accents. An oversized time ruler, scrubber and fault controls form a distinctive instrument. The scroll moves through time, freezes the failure and opens the underlying evidence.

Brand character: tactile, investigative, direct. More expressive, with greater motion and accessibility complexity.

Identity deliverables after direction selection: original logo/wordmark, monochrome and reverse SVGs, compact favicon, color and semantic tokens, licensed display/UI/data typography, icon rules, chart conventions, and a motion specification. A branch-and-return mark is a candidate to explore, not a final logo. Avoid snowflake clichés, generic network marks and decorative AI imagery.

The brand is FlakeMap. Replace Replay Harbor in public product naming and copy. The fictional Harbor Parts customer may remain because it is a sample organization. The former domain note does not establish anything about FlakeMap’s domain or trademark status.

06 / The demo must survive close inspection

Causal example: a stock read times out, causing a retry. In the candidate fixture, the retry succeeds and creates a draft, then the original late stock response also advances to drafting. The duplicate is caused by two completion paths lacking a shared idempotency guard—not by a timeout magically duplicating a purchase order. Show both paths and both draft artifacts.

  • Seed 8–12 fictional case records with linked source IDs, owners, chronological timestamps and a credible mix of pending, blocked, completed and rejected states.
  • Keep case count, run count, artifact count and review count separate. Derive all visible summaries from the same fixture data.
  • Expose baseline, candidate, fault, seed, run ID, expected artifact, actual artifacts, failed transition and reviewer state.
  • Support local mock annotation and report export; keep the workspace isolated from real data and third-party actions. Reset restores the exact initial fixtures and local review state.
  • A seed controls this fixture simulation only. It does not promise deterministic replay of external models, APIs or hidden production state.
  • Display “Simulation only; no production reliability estimate” beside aggregate metrics. Show the condition distribution, denominators and excluded/inconclusive cases. Avoid decorative confidence intervals or an opaque quality score.
  • Watermark reports and screenshots. Do not generate fake activity notifications, customer endorsements or live integration status.

Request form and analytics

Collect work email, role, company, workflow steps at a high level, existing evaluation stack, release cadence and whether trace sharing is approved. Include optional interview consent and separate explicit privacy consent. Do not accept raw traces, logs, contracts or secrets through the public form. If delivery is unconnected, show an honest local preview success state rather than saying a request was received.

Plan the named events: landing_view, demo_open, demo_complete, pricing_view, form_start, form_submit, verified_signup, interview_booked. Keep demo events separate with data_mode=synthetic; define event IDs, consent state, campaign fields and server-side dedupe before activation. Never fire verified signup or interview booked on a simulated success screen. Retention, deletion contact and the real data controller remain launch inputs.

07 / Build sequence and acceptance

  1. Visual selection: produce three image-based directions grounded in the references, then choose one. These research candidates are not yet design approval.
  2. Brand foundation: finish logo, favicon, palette, type, icons and motion rules before high-fidelity pages.
  3. Product model: author deterministic fixtures, causally coherent failure states and derived counts; then design the replay, matrix, review and report screens.
  4. Design: create the homepage chapters and complete module/page layouts, including 390px mobile and reduced-motion states. Figma is useful here when editable design files are requested; ImageGen is for art-direction exploration, not fake product screenshots.
  5. Implementation: use React/TypeScript with multi-page routing, semantic HTML and structured workflow graphics. Use CSS sticky and a scoped GSAP ScrollTrigger timeline for the central sequence. Lenis is optional, subject to preserving native navigation and accessibility; 3D is unnecessary for the initial concept.
  6. QA: test every CTA, menu, form/error/success state, filter, annotation, export and reset. Test 1440px, 390px, short viewports, keyboard focus, contrast, zoom, up/down scrolling and reduced motion. Inspect network activity to verify no fixture leakage or external effects.
  7. Evidence and handoff: capture real desktop/mobile renders of hero, core workflow, pricing, demo boundary and form success, plus narrow demo, queue/detail/evidence/report views. Annotate viewport and source. Deliver accessibility/performance findings, runbook, environment boundaries and experiment template. Publish and connect a domain in the later authorized deployment phase.

Performance guidance: avoid animation of layout, keep font/media loading out of the critical path, pause offscreen work and measure the actual build. These are acceptance requirements, not completed results. Animation performance; reduced-motion guidance; sticky storytelling pattern.

Demand experiment and decision record

Recruit three trace-owning engineering teams with authorized, anonymized artifacts. Agree one workflow and task-level criterion; compare observations against their existing gate and obtain independent confirmation. Advance only if a meaningful previously missed regression is confirmed and an engineering lead funds a trial or commits a real release gate. Otherwise narrow the proposition or stop.

Record experiment ID, owner, trace consent, stack, cadence, baseline gate, tested conditions, observed incremental regressions, independent reviewer, sponsor/budget owner, commitment evidence, costs, decision date and stop/go result. Unknown is distinct from zero. Proposed ad tests remain later work after form and consent QA; no ads or outreach were sent in this research phase.
